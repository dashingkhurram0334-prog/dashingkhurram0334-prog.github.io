Child Registration Certificate (CRC) 2026 — Requirements & Validity

Everything parents need to know about NADRA's Child Registration Certificate — who can apply, what's required, and how long it stays valid as your child grows.

Updated for 2026·4 min read

What Is a Child Registration Certificate?

The Child Registration Certificate (CRC) is an identity document issued by NADRA for children under the age of 18. It serves as a child's official record within NADRA's database before they become eligible to apply for their own CNIC at 18.

Who Can Apply

Either the mother or the father can apply for a CRC on behalf of their child. Only one parent needs to be present to submit the application, along with the required documents.

Documents Required

  • Computerized Birth Certificate: A birth certificate issued by the Union Council is the core requirement for a CRC application. Without it, the application cannot proceed.
  • Parent's CNIC: The applying parent (mother or father) must bring their original CNIC.
  • Child's presence (if 3 years or older): Children aged 3 and above must be physically brought to the NADRA center, since a photograph is required for validation at this age.

CRC Validity by Age

A CRC's validity and biometric requirements change as the child grows older. Here's how it breaks down:

Child's AgeWhat's RequiredValid Until
0 Month – 3 Years Birth certificate only — no photograph or biometric needed Up to age 3
3 Years – 10 Years Child must be present for a photograph Up to age 10
10 Years – 18 Years Photograph, fingerprint, and iris scan required Up to age 18
Note: Once a child turns 10, biometric capture (fingerprint and iris scan) becomes part of the CRC record alongside the photograph — this is different from the 1 month to 10 years stage, where only a photograph is needed once the child is 3 or older.

What Happens After 18?

Once the child turns 18, the CRC is no longer applicable. At this point, they become eligible to apply for their own CNIC (Computerized National Identity Card) directly, using their existing NADRA record as the base for the new application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do both parents need to be present to apply for a CRC?

No. Either the mother or the father can apply — only one parent needs to be present with the required documents.

Does a newborn need to be brought to the NADRA center?

No. Children under 3 years of age do not need to be physically present, since no photograph is required for this age group — only the computerized birth certificate is needed.

What happens when my child turns 10?

At age 10, the child's CRC record is updated to include a photograph along with a fingerprint and iris scan. This updated record remains valid until they turn 18.

Can a CRC be used instead of a CNIC for identification?

A CRC serves as a child's identity record with NADRA but is not a substitute for a CNIC. Once the individual turns 18, they must apply separately for a CNIC.

Is a Computerized Birth Certificate mandatory for a CRC?

Yes. A Computerized Birth Certificate issued by the Union Council is a mandatory requirement — the CRC application cannot be processed without it.
