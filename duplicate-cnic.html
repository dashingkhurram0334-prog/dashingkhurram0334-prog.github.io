Duplicate CNIC Issue — Resolving Two CNIC Numbers

PROBLEM & SOLUTION GUIDE · JULY 2026

Some Pakistani citizens discover they have two active CNIC numbers in NADRA's system. This unusual situation typically occurs due to administrative errors, data entry mistakes, or past application mishaps. Having duplicate CNICs can create legal and financial complications — but fortunately, NADRA has a formal process to consolidate and cancel one of them. This guide explains how to identify, report, and resolve duplicate CNIC issues.

⚠️ Important: Having two active CNIC numbers is a genuine problem, but it's not your fault as a citizen. NADRA handles such cases regularly. Do not delay in resolving this — it can affect bank accounts, property ownership, loan applications, and passport issuance.

Why Duplicate CNICs Happen

Duplicate CNIC numbers usually arise from a few common scenarios. Someone may have applied for a CNIC years ago, lost the application midway, and later reapplied — resulting in two separate CNIC numbers being issued. Data entry errors at NADRA centers can also create duplicate records (same person, slight name variation). Sometimes a lost or damaged CNIC was reported and replaced, but the old number wasn't properly cancelled in the system. Merging of databases or migration of old records can occasionally create unintended duplicates.

Unlike rejected applications or processing delays, duplicate CNICs require direct action at a NADRA center — they won't resolve on their own.

How to Discover if You Have Duplicate CNICs

Most people discover duplicates accidentally. You might apply for a passport and be told you have conflicting CNIC records. A bank might flag duplicate records when you open an account. You could try to renew your CNIC and discover the system shows two numbers. Or you might conduct a personal NADRA record check and find two entries.

If you suspect you have duplicates, visit any NADRA center and ask staff to run a search using your name, father's name, and date of birth. They can confirm whether one or two CNIC numbers exist in your name.

Documents Required to Resolve Duplicate CNIC

✓

Both CNIC Cards (Physical Copies): Bring both original CNIC cards if you have them. If either is lost or damaged, bring whatever documentation you have.

✓

Original CNIC: Your currently active and valid CNIC card (the one you use regularly).

✓

Proof of Identity: Passport, driver's license, or any other government-issued ID.

✓

Birth Certificate: Original or certified copy from the Union Council (to establish your singular identity).

✓

Educational Certificates: School certificates, college degrees, or any documents showing consistent name and identity across multiple records.

✓

Bank or Financial Records: Bank statements, loan documents, or utility bills that link to one CNIC number.

✓

Written Application: A formal letter to NADRA requesting consolidation of duplicate records and cancellation of one CNIC number. Specify which CNIC you want to keep and which to cancel.

Step-by-Step Resolution Process

1

Gather All Documents

Collect both CNIC cards (if available), your birth certificate, passport, educational certificates, and bank/utility documents. Prepare a formal written application requesting consolidation. Be clear about which CNIC number you want to keep (usually your currently active one).

2

Visit NADRA Headquarters or a Major Center

For duplicate CNIC cases, visit the main NADRA center in your city (typically the provincial headquarters center) rather than a small branch office. Smaller centers may not have authority to handle record consolidation. Call ahead or check nadra.gov.pk for the address of the main center in your city.

3

Request the Special Cases Counter

Upon arrival, ask staff to direct you to the "Special Cases" or "Administrative Resolution" counter. This counter handles unusual situations like duplicate records, identity disputes, and record corrections. Inform them you need to consolidate two CNIC numbers.

4

Submit Your Application and Documents

Present your written application along with all supporting documents. A NADRA officer will review everything and determine which CNIC is the legitimate primary record. They'll assess which number should remain active and which should be cancelled based on age of issuance, usage patterns, and documentation.

5

Biometric Verification

NADRA will take fresh fingerprints and iris scans to confirm you're one person, not two. This is a key step in consolidation — biometrics prove identity across both CNIC numbers.

6

Processing and Notification

The consolidation process typically takes 10-15 working days. One CNIC will be marked as cancelled in the system, and the other will remain active. You'll receive a formal letter from NADRA confirming the resolution. Keep this letter safely — you may need it for banking, property, or passport purposes.

What Happens to the Cancelled CNIC

Once NADRA cancels one CNIC number, that number becomes inactive in their system. It can no longer be used for any official purpose. You should destroy the physical card or keep it for personal records only. All banking, property, and government records should be updated to reflect only your remaining active CNIC number. Some banks and organizations may require you to notify them separately once the old number is cancelled.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Which CNIC number will they keep — the old one or the new one?
A: NADRA typically keeps whichever CNIC has been more actively used, has more recent issuance, or has stronger documentation supporting it. Usually, they keep your current, active CNIC and cancel the older or dormant one. You can express a preference in your application, though NADRA makes the final decision.
Q: Will this affect my bank account or property records?
A: Not negatively. Once the duplicate is resolved, banks and property records will reference only your active CNIC. However, after resolution, you may need to notify your bank to update their records if they have the old CNIC number on file. Property records typically don't need updates unless you're conducting new transactions.
Q: Do I have to pay a fee?
A: Resolving duplicate CNICs through the special cases counter is usually free if it's an administrative error by NADRA. However, if you're seeking to voluntarily cancel a secondary CNIC you yourself obtained, you may be charged a nominal fee (typically Rs. 500-1,000). Confirm at the center.
Q: How long does the whole process take?
A: From submission to resolution, 10-20 working days is typical. Some complex cases with missing documentation may take longer. Once NADRA notifies you of resolution, the cancelled CNIC is immediately inactive, even if it takes a few more days for the system to fully update everywhere.
Q: What if I lost both CNIC cards — can they still help?
A: Yes. Your CNIC exists in NADRA's digital system regardless of the physical card. Bring other identity documents (passport, birth certificate, educational records) and NADRA can still verify your identity and proceed with consolidation. You may want to apply for new cards of both numbers before consolidating, but this isn't always necessary.
Q: Can I do this online or must I visit in person?
A: For complex cases like duplicate consolidation, NADRA requires an in-person visit with original documents. This cannot be done online through the Pak-ID portal. You must physically visit a major NADRA center with all supporting documents.
Pro tip: If you're aware of duplicate CNICs affecting you, resolve it sooner rather than later. The longer you wait, the more complications can arise with banking, property ownership, loan applications, and international travel. Having it on record that NADRA has officially resolved and consolidated your duplicates protects you legally going forward.
