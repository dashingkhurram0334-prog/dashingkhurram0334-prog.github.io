CNIC Rejected? Here's How to Fix It

Common reasons NADRA rejects or holds an application, and the exact steps to resolve each one.

Last checked · 2026  ·  Independent Guide

Why Applications Get Rejected or Held

A rejected or held CNIC application is frustrating, but the vast majority of cases fall into a small number of predictable categories that can be resolved by understanding exactly what NADRA flagged during verification.

Common Rejection Reason 1: Missing Parent CNIC Link

If your father's or mother's CNIC information does not match the record already in NADRA's database — due to a spelling difference, an expired parent CNIC, or an unlinked family tree — your application can be held pending verification.

How to fix it: Provide the missing parent's CNIC for identification, or have the parent visit an NRC to confirm and update the family tree link before resubmitting.

Common Rejection Reason 2: Facial or Fingerprint Match Issues

Biometric mismatches occur when your fingerprint or facial scan does not clearly match prior records, which can happen due to worn fingerprints, poor lighting during capture, or a duplicate case already existing under a similar identity.

How to fix it: Identify and resolve facial match errors or duplicate cases by visiting the NRC in person, where staff can perform a manual re-scan and cross-check against the flagged record.

Common Rejection Reason 3: Document Mismatch or Inconsistency

Your provided information doesn't match across documents — for example, your name spelling differs on your birth certificate versus your passport, or your date of birth has a slight variation between records.

How to fix it: Carefully compare all your documents. If there's a genuine error on one (such as a typo on your birth certificate), get it corrected at the issuing authority first, then reapply with matching documents.

Common Rejection Reason 4: Age Requirement Not Met

You're under 18 years old — a full Smart CNIC is only issued to adults aged 18 and above.

How to fix it: Being under 18 doesn't leave you without options — you can apply for either a B-Form (Child Registration Certificate), a free paper-based document, or a Juvenile Card (JV), which carries an embedded chip and doubles as a plastic ID similar in format to an adult CNIC. Both work as valid proof of identity for a minor; the JV Card is simply the more secure, tamper-resistant option and is now often requested by schools and exam boards for verification purposes. Once you cross 18, you'll switch over to the standard Smart CNIC application.

Step-by-Step Resolution Process

  1. Visit your nearest NRC — Go to your closest NADRA Registration Center for a detailed review of the exact hold reason.
  2. Provide all valid documents — Bring birth certificates, parents' CNICs, and any supporting proofs relevant to the flagged issue.
  3. Verification or helpline support — Verify through your father or mother's record in person, or call NADRA support for guidance on documentation.

How to Contact NADRA for Help

Online Complaint: File a complaint online at complaints.nadra.gov.pk

What to Do If You're Rejected More Than Once

Repeated rejections for the same reason usually mean the underlying issue hasn't been fully resolved yet, rather than a new problem. Before resubmitting a second time, visit an NRC in person and ask staff to confirm — in writing if possible — exactly which document or field triggered the hold, so you don't end up correcting the wrong thing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a resubmission take after fixing the issue?

Once the flagged issue is resolved at the NRC, most applications resume normal processing within the original selected tier's timeframe.

Can I appeal a rejection without visiting an NRC?

For simple document corrections, sometimes yes through the online portal, but biometric mismatches almost always require an in-person visit.
