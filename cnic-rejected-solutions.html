CNIC Application Rejected? Why & How to Fix It

TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE · JULY 2026

Receiving a rejection notification for your CNIC application is frustrating, but it's not permanent. In most cases, rejections happen for fixable reasons — and you can reapply once you've corrected the issue. This guide explains the most common rejection reasons and gives you exact steps to resolve them and successfully reapply.

Why CNIC Applications Get Rejected

1. Document Mismatch or Inconsistency

This is the #1 reason for rejection. Your provided information doesn't match across documents — for example, your name spelling differs on your birth certificate versus your passport, or your date of birth has a slight variation.

How to fix: Carefully compare all your documents. If there's a genuine error on one document (e.g., a typo on your birth certificate), you'll need to get that document corrected at the issuing authority first. Once corrected, reapply with all matching documents.

2. Missing or Expired Documentation

You submitted documents that were either incomplete, illegible, or expired. For example, a passport that expired more than 6 months ago, or an address proof older than 3 months, or a birth certificate with missing signatures.

How to fix: Obtain fresh, valid documents. For address proof, get a recent utility bill (within 3 months). For birth certificates, get a certified copy from the Union Council. Renew your passport if needed. Once you have current documents, reapply.

3. Lineage Verification Failed

NADRA couldn't verify your family lineage. This happens when your parents' or guardians' information doesn't match NADRA records, or your birth wasn't properly registered in their system.

How to fix: First, verify that your parents hold valid CNICs. If they do, bring their CNIC copies to your next application. If one or both parents don't have CNICs or there's a registration gap, visit a NADRA center in person to file a special case. Bring as much family documentation as possible (old family records, school certificates, marriage certificates if applicable).

4. Age Requirement Not Met

You're under 18 years old. Only adults aged 18+ can apply for a Smart CNIC. Minors receive a B-Form instead.

How to fix: If you're under 18, apply for a B-Form (Child Registration Certificate) instead — it's free and serves as your identity document until you turn 18. Once you reach 18, apply for your Smart CNIC. No action needed right now except waiting.

5. Biometric Data Issues

Your fingerprints or iris scan was unclear or couldn't be captured properly, or there's a biometric inconsistency (for example, your fingerprints don't match previous records if you've applied before).

How to fix: Make sure your hands are clean and dry when you reapply. Avoid cuts, henna, or fresh tattoos on your fingers that might obscure prints. If you've applied before and there's a biometric mismatch, inform NADRA staff — they can check your old records and resolve the discrepancy.

6. Citizenship Status Unclear

NADRA couldn't confirm you're a Pakistani citizen. This may happen if you're applying as a naturalized citizen without proper documentation, or if your birth outside Pakistan wasn't properly registered.

How to fix: If you're a naturalized Pakistani, bring your naturalization certificate. If you were born abroad to Pakistani parents, bring your passport and your parents' CNICs. If documentation is complex, visit NADRA in person with all relevant papers — they can guide you through the special verification process.

7. Previous CNIC Record Found

NADRA found you already have a valid CNIC on file. You can't hold two active CNICs simultaneously. This can happen if someone with your name has a similar record, or if you previously applied and the card is still active.

How to fix: If you genuinely don't have a CNIC, visit NADRA in person with a NADRA officer to verify. If your old CNIC was lost or damaged, you should apply for a duplicate instead. If there's a record under a similar name, bring all identification to clear up the confusion.

Steps to Reapply Successfully

1

Identify the Exact Reason

Check your rejection notification carefully. NADRA typically specifies why your application was rejected. If the reason isn't clear, visit a NADRA center with your rejection slip to have staff explain it in detail.

2

Gather Corrected Documents

Based on the rejection reason, gather fresh or corrected documents. Ensure all information matches perfectly across documents — spelling of names, dates of birth, addresses, everything.

3

Visit a NADRA Center

Return to the same NADRA center (or any center if you prefer). Get a new token for a new application. Inform staff of your previous rejection so they can flag your case if necessary.

4

Re-submit with Extra Care

Ensure every document is clear, complete, and original (not photocopies for initial submission). Double-check all information with the staff before they finalize your application.

5

Track Your New Application

Use your new reference number to track the second application. Rejections are rarely repeated if you've genuinely fixed the issue.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: How long before I can reapply after rejection?
A: You can reapply immediately — there's no waiting period. As soon as you've corrected the issue, visit NADRA and submit a new application.
Q: Do I have to pay the fee again?
A: Yes, each application is a separate transaction. You'll pay the full fee (Normal, Urgent, or Executive) again. However, if the rejection was NADRA's error, they may waive the fee — ask at the center.
Q: Can rejection affect my future applications?
A: No, one rejection doesn't prevent future applications. NADRA sees rejections as part of the normal process. Reapply as many times as needed until approved.
Q: What if I can't fix the issue myself (e.g., parents' lineage)?
A: NADRA has a special cases department for complex situations. Visit your local center in person with all available documentation. Staff can guide you through alternative verification routes.
Q: Can I apply at a different NADRA center?
A: Yes, you can apply at any NADRA center in the country. Your previous rejection won't bar you from applying elsewhere, though your records are nationwide anyway.
Still stuck? If you've reapplied and face continued rejection for unclear reasons, or if your situation is complex, visit a NADRA center in person to speak with a supervisor. They can review your full case history and sometimes offer solutions that aren't available through the standard reapplication process. Alternatively, contact NADRA's helpline at 051-111-786-100 to ask about special cases or dispute procedures.
