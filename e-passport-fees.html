What Is the e-Passport?

Pakistan's e-Passport is a chip-embedded travel document that stores the holder's biometric data digitally, replacing the older machine-readable passport (MRP). It is issued by the Directorate General of Immigration and Passports (DGIP) and is designed to meet international ICAO standards for enhanced security at border crossings.

Step-by-Step Application Process

  1. Register on the official DGIP online portal
  2. Fill in the passport application form and select your preferred validity, page count, and processing tier
  3. Upload your CNIC and photograph
  4. Book an appointment at your nearest Passport Office
  5. Pay the fee online or via designated bank branches
  6. Visit for biometric verification and document submission
Choosing a 10-year validity passport works out cheaper per year than renewing a 5-year passport twice, making it the better value option for frequent travelers.

Choosing the Right Processing Tier

Pakistan's e-Passport system offers three processing speeds, and picking the right one depends on how soon you actually need the passport rather than simply choosing the fastest option available. Normal processing is the most economical choice and works well if you're applying well ahead of any planned travel — for example, renewing a passport that's still valid for a few more months, or applying for a first passport without an immediate trip planned.

Urgent processing suits situations where travel is expected within the next month or two but isn't an immediate emergency — a confirmed visa appointment, an upcoming conference, or a family event abroad. Fast Track is generally reserved for genuine time-sensitive cases: a sudden travel requirement, a medical emergency abroad involving a family member, or a visa deadline that leaves no room for the standard processing window.

Why Each Document Is Required

DGIP's document requirements aren't arbitrary — each one serves a specific verification purpose in the application process:

Valid original CNIC: Since the e-Passport pulls your biographical data directly from NADRA's database, your CNIC must be current and error-free. Any mismatch between your CNIC details and what you enter on the passport application can cause processing delays, so it's worth double-checking your CNIC information beforehand, especially if you've had a recent name, address, or marital status change.

Previous passport, if renewing: This allows DGIP to verify your travel history and confirm there are no outstanding issues (such as a lost passport report) tied to your record before issuing a new one.

Passport-sized photograph: Even though the e-Passport process is largely digital, DGIP still requires a photograph meeting specific size and background requirements for identity verification and printing purposes.

Proof of profession: This is typically requested for specific applicant categories — such as government employees, students, or those applying under certain visa-sponsored categories — where DGIP needs to verify occupational status as part of the application.

Renewing vs. First-Time Application — What's Different

A first-time passport application generally takes slightly longer to process than a renewal, since DGIP is verifying your identity and CNIC details from scratch rather than updating an existing record. First-time applicants should also expect the biometric verification step to take a bit longer, as there's no prior fingerprint or photo record on file to cross-reference against.

Renewals, by contrast, are usually faster because most of your biographical data is already verified in the system — the main checks involve confirming your CNIC is still valid and that your appearance/photo is reasonably current. If your CNIC itself needs updating (address change, expired card, and so on), it's worth resolving that first, since an outdated CNIC is one of the most common reasons renewal applications get delayed.

Common Reasons for Application Delays

A few recurring issues tend to slow down e-Passport applications:

  • CNIC and application data mismatch: Even small discrepancies — a differently spelled name, an old address still on file — can trigger manual review and add processing time.
  • Incomplete biometric verification: If fingerprint or photo capture at the Passport Office isn't completed properly, you may be asked to return for a second visit.
  • Payment verification delays: Bank-based payments sometimes take a business day or two to reflect in DGIP's system, which can push back your appointment slot if not accounted for.
  • High-volume periods: Processing times listed are typical estimates — during peak travel seasons or after public holidays, even Fast Track applications can take slightly longer than usual due to higher application volume.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I upgrade my processing tier after submitting my application?

In most cases, yes — you can visit the Passport Office and request an upgrade to a faster tier by paying the fee difference, though this depends on how far your application has already progressed.

What happens if my passport application is rejected?

DGIP will typically notify you of the specific reason — often a document or CNIC data issue — which you'll need to resolve before resubmitting. Fees already paid are generally not refunded for a rejected application, so it's worth double-checking your documents beforehand.

Do children need a separate e-Passport, or can they be added to a parent's?

Pakistan does not allow children to be added to a parent's passport — every individual, regardless of age, requires their own separate passport application with an original CRC or B-Form as supporting documentation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can overseas Pakistanis apply for an e-Passport abroad?

Yes, applications can be submitted through Pakistani embassies and consulates, though fees may be charged in the local currency equivalent.

What's the difference between Urgent and Fast Track?

Fast Track offers the quickest turnaround, often same-week collection, at a higher fee than the Urgent tier.
