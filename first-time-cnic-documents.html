First-Time CNIC Documents Checklist (2026)

Applying for a CNIC for the first time trips people up more often on missing paperwork than on anything else. Most delays trace back to one of a handful of predictable gaps — a birth certificate that was never obtained, a parent's card that expired years ago and was never renewed, or a family record that doesn't quite match what the applicant is claiming. Here's a clear breakdown of exactly what's needed, and for which situation, based on how NADRA's own registration process actually works.

The One Document Everyone Needs First

Before anything else, a first-time applicant needs a Union Council-issued, computerized birth certificate. This is treated as a non-negotiable starting point — without it, a NADRA center will not begin processing a first-time CNIC application at all, except in a narrow set of exceptional circumstances handled separately. If you don't already have this certificate, get it from your local Union Council office before visiting a NADRA center, not after.

Two Types of First-Time Applicants

NADRA treats first-time CNIC applicants as falling into two distinct groups, and which group you're in determines how you apply.

Group 1 — Already has a B-Form. If a Child Registration Certificate (B-Form) was already issued for you at some point, along with its own B-Form number, you can apply for your first CNIC directly through the Pak Identity mobile app. There's no need to visit a center in person for this group.

Group 2 — No B-Form was ever issued. If a B-Form was never obtained and the applicant has already turned 18, the case needs to be handled more carefully at a physical NADRA center rather than through the app, since additional family verification is usually required.

Standard Requirements for a First-Time CNIC

For a straightforward case — where the applicant's Union Council birth certificate is in hand and at least one parent is alive, holds a valid CNIC, and is registered as married to the other parent — no further documents are typically required beyond the birth certificate itself. The presence of at least one parent in NADRA's records, with their marriage properly registered, is usually enough to establish the family link needed to process the application.

Applying for a Fresh (First-Time) CNIC: The Process

A fresh CNIC application follows six simple steps at your nearest NADRA Registration Center. First, visit the center and obtain a token from the kiosk or reception. Next, submit your documents — primarily your Union Council Computerized Birth Certificate, along with your original Form 'B' and an attested copy if one has already been issued. Once verified, you'll go through biometric enrollment, which includes a photograph, fingerprint scan, and iris capture where available. Your application then goes through a verification stage to confirm your family record, followed by fee payment based on your chosen processing tier — Normal, Urgent, or Executive. Once payment is complete, your card enters processing and can be tracked using your token number.

When a Parent Is Unavailable

Not every applicant has both parents alive, present, or registered. NADRA's process accounts for several of these situations directly:

  • One parent is deceased or unavailable: The surviving parent's CNIC and registered marriage record are generally sufficient to proceed.
  • Neither parent is available: If a sibling of the applicant already has a CNIC and the family relationship is established in NADRA's system, that sibling can support the application.
  • Neither parents nor siblings are available, and the applicant is under 18: A court-issued Guardianship Certificate is required. This document — commonly obtained when a relative such as an uncle, aunt, or grandparent has taken on responsibility for the child — must be brought to the NADRA center along with the birth certificate.
  • Neither parents nor siblings are available, and the applicant is 18 or older: A Guardianship Certificate is not required in this case, since the applicant is a legal adult. Instead, the family link can be established through a paternal or maternal relative — such as an uncle — whose relationship to the applicant's deceased parent is already on record with NADRA (for example, listed together in the same Family Registration Certificate).

Common Mistakes That Cause Delays

A small number of recurring issues account for most of the delays first-time applicants run into. Knowing them ahead of time can save an unnecessary trip back to the center.

  • Applying without the Union Council birth certificate. This is treated as a mandatory document, not an optional one — applications are generally not processed without it.
  • A deceased parent's CNIC was never cancelled. If a parent has passed away but their CNIC record was never formally cancelled with NADRA, it can create inconsistencies that need to be resolved before the application can move forward.
  • Inconsistent family details across records. If different family members have recorded slightly different versions of a parent's name across their own CNIC applications over the years, it can create a mismatch that needs to be clarified at the counter.
  • An expired family member's CNIC. If a parent's or sibling's CNIC being used to establish the family link has expired, it generally needs to be renewed first before it can support a new application.

Despite these possible complications, the large majority of first-time applications — NADRA estimates roughly 90–95% — go through without any issue at all, particularly as more applicants have become aware of what documentation to prepare in advance.

Using the Pak Identity App for a First-Time CNIC

For applicants who already have a B-Form on record, the Pak Identity app allows the entire first-time CNIC process to be handled without visiting a center. Through the app, you can also check your family composition, see your application's tracking status, and review your own verification history — including where and when your CNIC has previously been used for identity checks.

One detail worth being careful about: the app is meant strictly for personal use. Logging into someone else's account, or letting someone else use your login to process their own application, is not permitted, and shared or multiple logins have been restricted specifically to prevent this. Use only your own account, or that of an immediate family member you're directly assisting — a child, spouse, or parent.

What to Do If Your Family's CNICs Have Expired

If any family member's CNIC used to support your application has expired, it needs to be renewed before it can be relied on for a first-time application. The Pak Identity app displays the CNIC expiry date of every family member listed under your family composition, making it easy to check this in advance rather than discovering the problem at the counter.

Where to Get Help If You're Unsure

If there's any uncertainty about which category your case falls into, calling NADRA's helpline at 1777 before visiting a center can save a wasted trip. Being ready to explain your specific situation — whether a parent is alive or deceased, whether a death certificate exists, and whether a Union Council birth certificate has already been obtained — allows the helpline or the counter officer to guide you to the right documentation for your case.

Questions can also be raised through NADRA's official, verified Facebook page, or by emailing NADRA's support desk at csd@nadra.gov.pk. Information shared through unofficial groups or pages not affiliated with NADRA should be treated with caution, since anyone can respond there regardless of whether they have any actual authority on the subject.
