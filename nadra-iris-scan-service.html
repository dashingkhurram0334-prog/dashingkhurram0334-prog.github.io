Biometric

NADRA Iris Scan Service

NADRA has been quietly rolling out a third layer of biometric identification across its registration centers — iris scanning. If you've noticed a small camera-and-screen device sitting next to the fingerprint scanner at your local NADRA office and wondered what it does, here's a plain-language look at what iris capture is, why NADRA added it, and who actually needs to use it.

From Two Biometric Markers to Three

For years, NADRA verified identity using two biometric markers: fingerprints and facial recognition. Fingerprints were the original standard, and facial matching was added later as a backup — useful for the small number of people whose fingerprints are faint, worn down from manual labor, or otherwise difficult for a scanner to read.

Iris scanning is the third marker in this system. It captures the unique pattern in the colored part of the eye, which — unlike fingerprints — stays essentially unchanged for most of a person's life. NADRA introduced this capability after amendments to the NADRA Rules 2002 formally recognized iris capture as part of the legal definition of biometric data, and it has since expanded to counters across a growing number of centers nationwide, with tens of millions of iris records already captured.

Why Iris Scanning Matters Most for Children

The clearest case for iris scanning is child registration. Children under 18 make up nearly half of Pakistan's registered population, and their physical features change rapidly. A three-year-old's fingerprints are not yet fully formed and are unreliable for matching. A face photographed at age three looks very different by age nine or eleven, as the child's features develop.

This creates a real identity-security gap: without a stable biometric marker, children's records were historically easier to duplicate or misuse, since there was little reliable way to confirm that a name and a face still belonged to the same child years later. Iris patterns, by contrast, are established by around age two and remain stable for decades, giving NADRA a dependable way to anchor a child's identity to their Child Registration Certificate (CRC) even as the rest of their appearance changes.

Because of this, iris capture is now built into the CRC application process. When a Child Registration Certificate is applied for, the device photographs the child and captures an iris scan as part of the same biometric enrollment — at no additional cost beyond the standard CRC fee. Iris capture is not currently mandatory for every application channel; applications submitted purely through the Pak Identity app, for example, do not include an iris scan, while applications processed at a physical NADRA center with the sensor present do.

Self-Service Kiosk Machines at NADRA Centers

Alongside the iris sensors, many centers now have ATM-style self-service kiosks. These allow a citizen whose biometric details haven't changed — no update to their name, address, or family particulars — to walk through a straightforward identity-renewal process on their own: verifying fingerprints or face, choosing a delivery address, and generating a QR code used to pay the applicable fee, without needing a counter appointment. An on-site facilitator is typically available to guide first-time users through the screen.

These self-service machines are being expanded to more cities over time, aimed at reducing counter congestion — particularly in high-traffic Union Council areas — by giving routine renewal applicants a faster, independent path through the process.

Mobile Registration Tablets

NADRA has also deployed portable tablet devices, built domestically, that carry the same core software as the Pak Identity platform. These tablets can capture fingerprints, photographs, and iris scans in the field, letting registration teams travel to schools and Union Council offices to handle child registration and family-record updates — such as recording a birth, marriage, or divorce — without requiring families to travel to a fixed center.

Is There an Extra Fee for Iris Scanning?

No. Iris capture is included within the standard fee for the identity document being processed — whether that's a CNIC or a Child Registration Certificate. NADRA does not charge a separate line-item fee for the iris scan itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do adults need to get an iris scan too?
Adults are not required to undergo a separate iris enrollment. The feature is primarily built around strengthening child identity records, though the underlying biometric system now supports all three markers — fingerprint, facial, and iris — for future verification needs.

What happens if the iris scanner isn't available at my center?
Not every NADRA center or counter currently has the iris sensor installed. If it isn't present, your application proceeds using the standard fingerprint and facial biometric process; the iris scan is an additional layer where available, not a blocking requirement.

Can I use the self-service kiosk for a new CNIC application?
Self-service kiosks are intended for straightforward renewals where no personal details have changed. Applications involving corrections, first-time registration, or changes to family particulars still need to go through a standard counter.
