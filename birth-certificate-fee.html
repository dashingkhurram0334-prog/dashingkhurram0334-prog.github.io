Birth Certificate Fee 2026: Free (Punjab) to Rs. 1,500 by Province

Last checked · 2026  ·  Independent Guide

A birth certificate is the very first identity document a child in Pakistan needs — and the fee depends heavily on where you register. Punjab currently waives the fee entirely for early registration, while other provinces and Islamabad charge Rs. 200 to Rs. 1,500 depending on the child's age and how quickly you register. This guide breaks down the fee by region, explains who actually issues the certificate, and walks through the full process.

Who Issues the Birth Certificate — NADRA or Union Council?

A common misconception is that NADRA directly issues birth certificates. It does not. Birth registration in Pakistan is handled by local government bodies — Union Councils, Municipal Committees, or Cantonment Boards, depending on where the birth took place. In Karachi specifically, registration is handled through the relevant Union Committee.

NADRA's role comes in afterward: your Union Council-issued birth certificate is what NADRA uses to generate your child's B-Form (Child Registration Certificate). Most Union Councils have now moved to an online system, generating certificates only after approval is confirmed through NADRA's central database — but the certificate itself is a local government document, not a NADRA one.

Outside Punjab, registration within 60 days typically carries the standard Rs. 200 fee shown above, with processing taking approximately 7 working days once documents are verified.

Documents Required

  • Hospital birth notification slip, or the child's vaccination (Safety/Immunization) card, if the child was born in a hospital
  • A signed and attested affidavit confirming the birth, if the child was born at home
  • CNIC of both parents (a close relative's CNIC may be used instead, if a parent cannot be present)
  • Parents' marriage certificate (Nikah Nama), if requested
  • Completed birth registration form from the relevant Union Council

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Visit the Union Council, Municipal Committee, or Cantonment Board office covering the area where the child was born
  2. Submit the birth registration form along with the required documents
  3. Pay the applicable fee at the counter
  4. Officials verify the submitted details against parental records
  5. Once approved, the certificate is generated — many Union Councils now issue it online through NADRA's verification system

Why Registering Early Matters

Registering a birth immediately is one of the most important administrative steps a parent can take. Without a birth certificate, a child cannot be issued a B-Form, which in turn is required for school admission, a future passport, and eventually their own CNIC at age 18. Delayed registration tends to compound — the longer it's left, the more documentation is typically required to prove the child's details.

How to Register a Child's Birth Certificate (CBRC)

Registering a child's birth within 60 days of birth is legally required in Pakistan. This registration is the first step toward establishing your child's legal identity, and it is required before applying for a Form B or CNIC later.

Important: Before applying for a birth certificate, both the husband and wife's marital status must be updated as "Married" in NADRA's records. If this update has not been made for either parent, the birth certificate application may not be processed.

Step-by-Step Registration Process at the Union Council

  1. One of the parents, or a close relative, must visit the relevant Union Council in person to register the child's birth.
  2. Bring the required documents (hospital slip/vaccination card, or affidavit for home births).
  3. The Union Council staff will verify the submitted documents.
  4. Once the documents are confirmed correct, the staff will enter the birth record into their system.
  5. A proof print of the application will be issued for review — check all details carefully.
  6. Sign the proof print and return it to the Union Council staff.
  7. Upon return, the Union Council will issue the CBRC (Computerized Birth Registration Certificate) or Computerized Birth Certificate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does NADRA issue birth certificates?

No. Birth certificates are issued by local Union Councils, Municipal Committees, or Cantonment Boards — not directly by NADRA. NADRA uses this certificate to generate the B-Form (CRC) afterward.

How much does a birth certificate cost in Pakistan?

It depends on your province. Punjab currently offers free registration and certificate issuance for children up to 7 years old. Islamabad (ICT) charges Rs. 200 to Rs. 1,500 depending on age and processing speed. Other provinces typically charge Rs. 200 for standard Union Council registration.

Is a birth certificate really free in Punjab?

Yes — under the Punjab Birth & Death Registration Rules, registration and the computerized certificate are free of charge for children up to 7 years old. Late registration beyond that age may require a newspaper advertisement and applicable administrative fees.

How long does it take to get a birth certificate?

Typically around 7 working days after submission and verification of documents, though this can vary by location.

Can I apply for a birth certificate online?

Online registration is available in some cities through local government digitization initiatives, but availability depends on your specific area — check with your local Union Council.

Is there a PDF form available for Union Council birth registration?

Most Union Councils use a standard printed registration form available at the office itself. Some provinces are moving toward online/app-based registration, reducing the need for a physical form.

Is there a deadline to register a child's birth?

Yes. Registering a child's birth within 60 days of birth is a legal requirement in Pakistan.

Who can go to the Union Council to register the birth?

Either parent, or a close relative, can visit the Union Council in person along with the required documents to complete the registration.
